A trust protocol for Kenya's everyday economy

Protected trade, chama-style capital, community groups with rules that cannot bend, and reputation nobody can buy — built as a small frozen set of on-chain primitives plus an open engine any approved app can drive. These pages are for the people building on it: what exists, what is planned, and exactly how money and facts move.
The one design law: the chain never knows what a "mango" is. No contract names a vertical; a new business type is a schema registration — a transaction, never a deployment. Every other rule on these pages follows from that one.

The whole stack in one picture

A phone signs; an app backend relays through the open engine; the contracts hold every shilling and every fact; the indexer turns the event log back into books and reputation. Nothing the company runs can move user money on its own.

Contract layer

Fifteen contracts in four layers, wired through a single hub so any one of them can be replaced with one parameter change. Six playable flows: a protected trade and its waterfall, the dispute branch, a chama's whole lifecycle, a funding pool, a lost phone, and governance with its money taps.

Built (on a local chain today)

  • 15 contracts · 146 forge tests · reference backend + reference app
  • Escrow with a 2% fee ceiling in code; funding-pool skim; negotiated commits
  • Per-person vaults (recoverable, not seizable); community groups with quorum treasuries
  • Nine end-to-end suites; live invariants audit that refuses to trust the database over the chain

Planned

  • Testnet broadcast (Celo Sepolia) → audit → mainnet behind UUPS + timelock
  • Concurrent project pools; milestone tranches unlocked by field attestations
  • Dispute juror incentives; uniqueness/KYC attestation network
  • Engine: event-stream push, micro-trade batching, FFI, multi-chain
